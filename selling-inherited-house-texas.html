Selling an Inherited House in Texas: A Step-by-Step Guide

A practical guide for heirs, executors and families navigating probate, property condition, title and a potential home sale in Texas.

By Lisa Williams, REALTOR® | eXp Realty, LLC

Inheriting a home can raise difficult questions: Who has authority to sell? Do you have to complete probate first? Can you sell the house as-is? The answers depend on how the property was owned, the estate documents and Texas law. This guide explains the real estate decisions to discuss with your probate attorney and title company.

Important: This is general educational information, not legal, tax or financial advice. A Texas probate attorney should determine who has authority to act for an estate, and a title company should confirm what is needed to insure a sale. Rules and timelines vary by situation.

Step 1: Confirm who has legal authority to sell an inherited house in Texas

Being a family member or named beneficiary does not automatically mean you can sign a listing agreement or deed for an estate-owned home. Authority may come from a court-appointed executor or administrator, a properly documented transfer, or other legally recognized circumstances. Texas estates can involve independent administration, dependent administration, a muniment of title, or other procedures. The correct route depends on the facts.

Before marketing the property, ask a Texas probate attorney who holds title, whether court approval is required, and who may sign the sale documents. If there are multiple heirs, clarify how decisions will be made.

Step 2: Gather documents for the inherited property sale

Start with the documents available to you, then let the attorney and title company identify anything else needed. Useful records may include:

  • Death certificate and any will or trust documents
  • Probate court orders and letters testamentary or letters of administration, when applicable
  • Current deed, mortgage statement and property tax records
  • HOA information, insurance details and available repair history
  • Information about liens, outstanding debts or ownership interests

Do not assume every estate needs the same paperwork. The title company will determine its underwriting requirements for the specific transaction.

Step 3: Assess the inherited home's condition and carrying costs

An inherited house may have deferred maintenance, personal belongings, vacant-property risks or utility issues. Make a practical inventory of safety concerns, roof and foundation conditions, major systems, insurance coverage, taxes and monthly expenses. Protect the property from avoidable damage while decisions are pending.

If you are selling an inherited house in Conroe, The Woodlands, Montgomery County, Travis County or Galveston County, local comparable sales and property condition will matter more than an online estimate alone.

Step 4: Decide whether to sell the inherited house as-is or renovate

Selling as-is may reduce preparation time and upfront spending, but buyers may discount their offers for repairs and risk. Making targeted repairs may attract a wider pool of buyers, but not every improvement pays for itself. Compare expected sale price, repair costs, time on market, holding expenses and your family's capacity to manage the work.

Compare as-is versus renovation scenarios with our calculator. Calculator results are estimates, not appraisals or guarantees.

Step 5: Build a realistic pricing and marketing plan

A comparative market analysis considers recent nearby sales, active competition, location, condition and features. A thoughtful estate home marketing plan should identify the likely buyer audience, appropriate property preparation, showing arrangements and how offers will be evaluated. Confirm with the estate's authorized representative before making commitments.

For a starting estimate of transaction costs, try the Texas Seller Net Proceeds Calculator. Actual estate proceeds depend on debts, taxes, fees, closing costs and legal distributions.

Step 6: Coordinate title, contracts and closing

Before accepting an offer, have the attorney and title company review signing authority, vesting, liens and any required approvals. The real estate agent coordinates marketing, offers, contract deadlines and closing logistics; the attorney handles legal questions about the estate and heirs. The title company addresses title insurance and closing requirements.

After closing, the disposition of funds is an estate-administration matter. Do not assume sale proceeds can be distributed immediately or equally among heirs.

Frequently asked questions about selling inherited property in Texas

Can you sell an inherited house before probate is finished?

Sometimes a sale can proceed during an estate administration, but only with the necessary authority and approvals. Whether probate is required at all depends on the ownership and estate circumstances. Ask a Texas probate attorney and title company before listing.

Can multiple heirs sell a house in Texas?

Potentially, yes. The required signatures and process depend on how title is held and whether an estate representative has authority. Disagreements among heirs can delay a sale and may require legal resolution.

Can an executor sell a Texas house as-is?

An authorized executor may be able to sell an estate property as-is, subject to applicable duties, required approvals and contract terms. “As-is” does not automatically eliminate disclosure duties or protect against misrepresentation. Ask your attorney about the estate's specific obligations.

Do you pay capital gains tax when selling inherited property?

Tax treatment depends on the property's tax basis, value at death, improvements, selling costs and other facts. Inherited property often receives a basis adjustment under federal tax rules, but exceptions exist. Consult a qualified tax professional before estimating taxes.

How long does it take to sell an inherited home in Texas?

There is no universal timeline. Legal authority, title requirements, property condition, family decisions and market demand can all affect the schedule. A listing plan can be developed while the estate's legal requirements are clarified.
