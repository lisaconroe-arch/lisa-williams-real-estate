The Executor's Property Guide

A practical starting point for the real estate side of an inherited home - what to protect first, what not to rush, who handles which decisions, and how to prepare for a future sale without assuming every estate follows the same path.

First: you do not have to solve the entire house today.

Serving as an executor or other estate representative can mean dealing with a home at the same time you're dealing with grief, family questions, paperwork and deadlines. From the real estate side, the first goal is usually not to start remodeling or put a sign in the yard. It is to understand the property's condition, protect it, and make sure the appropriate legal and title questions are being handled by the professionals responsible for them.

Important: This guide is real estate education, not legal advice. Texas estates can be administered in different ways, and authority to sell can depend on the will, court orders, type of administration and other facts. Your probate attorney and title company should address legal authority and title requirements.

A practical property-first checklist

Secure and protect the property

Confirm the home is reasonably secure, utilities and insurance are being addressed by the appropriate person, and urgent issues such as active leaks or weather exposure are not being ignored. Avoid disposing of property or making major changes merely because the home appears vacant.

Identify the legal decision-maker

Do not assume that being a child, sibling, beneficiary or occupant automatically gives someone authority to sell. Let the estate's attorney and title professionals determine who has authority and what documentation is required for the particular estate.

Understand the house before spending money

A real estate assessment can help identify condition issues, likely buyer expectations and comparable market activity. That information can help frame the conversation about selling as-is versus investing in repairs or preparation.

Inventory the property-facing decisions

Furniture, personal belongings, deferred maintenance, vehicles, storage, lawn care and clean-out can all affect the listing timeline. The real estate plan should respect legal instructions about estate property rather than assuming everything in the house can be removed.

Build the listing around the estate

Once the appropriate authority and title path are understood, the listing strategy can address pricing, preparation, photography, marketing, showings, offers and the contract-to-closing timeline.

Who handles what?

Probate / Estate Attorney

Legal authority, probate administration, court requirements, interpretation of wills or orders, disputes among interested parties and other legal questions.

Title Company

Title examination, requirements for insurability and closing, payoff and lien matters within its role, closing documents and settlement coordination.

Lisa - Real Estate

Property and market assessment, comparable-market information, preparation strategy, listing and marketing, showings, buyer feedback, offers and real estate contract coordination through closing.

As-is or make repairs?

There is no automatic answer. A home needing work can still be marketable. Before spending estate funds on cosmetic improvements, it can be useful to compare the likely cost, time and market impact of the work against an as-is strategy. Some repairs may improve marketability; others may not return what they cost.

Questions I look at from the real estate side

What condition are competing homes in? Which defects are likely to affect financing or buyer confidence? What is the probable buyer pool? How much time and coordination would repairs require? Is the goal maximum retail exposure, speed, simplicity, or some balance among them?

Before the first listing conversation

You don't need a perfect file. Helpful starting information includes the property address, who is currently caring for the property, whether it is occupied, the general condition, any known mortgage or major property issues, where the estate is in its process, and the attorney/title contacts if appropriate.
